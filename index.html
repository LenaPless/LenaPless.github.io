About Me

I am a third-year PhD student at the Center for Astrophysics and Supercomputing at Swinburne University of Technology, working on the gas conditions of galaxies at intermediate redshifts using data from the James Webb Space Telescope. I'm particularly interested in finding good analogs for the first low-mass galaxies that kicked off the Epoch of Reionization to better help us understand how the Universe underwent its last major phase transition.
I have constructed an expansive grid of emission line strengths using the photoionization code cloudy based on the BPASS stellar population models dependent on important interstellar medium quantities such as the metallicity, density, and ionization parameter. By fitting observed emission lines to this grid, I can then constrain the gas conditions in galaxies.

Are High-EW LAEs good analogs for the earliest galaxies?
A study on the ionizing conditions and gas properties of LAEs at 2 ≲ z ≲ 7

Lyman-α emitters (LAEs) are frequently proposed as analogs of the low-mass, metal-poor galaxies thought to dominate cosmic reionization. In particular, galaxies with large Lyα equivalent widths (EWs) are expected to have not experienced significant chemical enrichment. We test this hypothesis using a sample of 88 LAEs at 2 ≲ z ≲ 7, selected from MUSE observations that are undetected in deep HST imaging and subsequently followed up with JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy. Despite the unprecedented sensitivity of JWST, only 50 of the LAEs exhibit secure counterparts in available deep NIRCam imaging reaching typical 3σ depths of mAB ∼ 30.3mag, highlighting the intrinsically faint nature of the population. Gas-phase metallicities, ionization parameters, and hydrogen densities are inferred by fitting optical emission lines with a grid of BPASS+cloudy photoionization models, while stellar masses are derived from NIRCam photometry through SED fitting. The final sample with secure stellar masses and gas-phase metallicities encompasses only 16 LAEs, occupying regions of standard emission-line diagnostic diagrams consistent with highly ionized, low-metallicity star-forming systems. Although the strong-line metallicity diagnostic R3 appears to decrease with increasing Lyα EW, we find no compelling evidence for a strong monotonic relation between Lyα EW and gas-phase metallicity when metallicities are inferred through full emission-line fitting. However, galaxies with stronger Lyα emission tend to lie below the average mass-metallicity relation at fixed stellar masses, suggesting that they are preferentially less chemically evolved than typical galaxies of the same mass. These results indicate that Lyα EW alone may not uniquely identify the most metal-poor systems. We conclude that high-EW LAEs provide useful, though imperfect, analogs of the galaxies thought to drive cosmic reionization, and combining Lyα EW with stellar mass may provide a more effective route for identifying the most chemically primitive galaxies
